Concordia turns source metadata and bounded observations into a reviewable Universal Schema Language (USL). It combines deterministic probes, normalization rules, type and label checks, and optional adversarial review to distinguish supported relationships from uncertain or misleading joins. The system preserves alternatives and corrections in an auditable ledger, then compiles only eligible relationships into a USL. Its reports make sample limits, cost, and uncertainty visible: a passing bounded check is not a guarantee about a whole dataset, and schema correspondence alone is not a foreign key. The project demonstrates Zetaris onboarding and USL workflows, with H-MEM-backed decision preservation and optional Meterless/Nightshift text assistance. Public examples are synthetic; benchmark raw data and gold files are not redistributed.
